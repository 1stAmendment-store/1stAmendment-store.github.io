Privacy Policy

App: Magic 8-Ball
Developer: 1stAmendment
Last updated: 14 October 2026

The short version

Magic 8-Ball keeps your movie and TV data on your device. It has no analytics of its own and does not track you across other companies' apps. It does not ask for your name, your location, your contacts or your camera. It uses your photos only when you ask it to: to read the one photo you pick as a profile picture, and to save a wallpaper you made into your photos.

Ads are optional. You can watch a short ad to get an extra spin once your free spins for the hour are used. The ad network is contacted only from that point, and the ads are not personalized to you (see "Advertising" below).

Accounts are optional. The app works fully without one. If you create one — with a username and password, with Apple, or with Google Play Games — it is held by Unity Gaming Services, which also collects some data about how the sign-in service is used (see below). If you never open the Account screen, the app never contacts Unity at all. You can delete your account from inside the app at any time.

Friends and chat are optional too. With an account you can add friends by their friend name and chat with them — typed messages, and movies and TV shows shared from your lists. Messages go only between friends, and are held by Unity's chat service so you can see earlier ones (see "Friends and chat" below).

Amazon links are optional. A film's Buy and Rent buttons open Amazon, and we may earn a commission if you buy something there (see "Amazon links" below).

Apart from an account and any ad you choose to watch, the only information the app sends is the searches and lookups for movies, TV shows and people it makes to The Movie Database (TMDB) in order to show them to you. Your own notes, reviews and ratings are never sent anywhere — not even to your account.

What the app stores on your device

All of this is saved locally, in the app's private storage. None of it is transmitted to us or to anyone else.

Your movie and TV library

  • Films and TV shows you have rolled, saved, collected or marked as watched (only films are rolled; shows are added by searching)
  • Details about them fetched from TMDB — title, year, poster, synopsis, director or creator, genres, where they stream, and for a show how many seasons it has
  • Anything you write or choose yourself: your personal rating, your review, your notes, the format and condition of a copy you own (for a TV show, which seasons the copy holds and the condition of each), whether you have excluded a film, and — for a TV show you have watched — which seasons and episodes you watched and the star rating you gave each episode

Your settings

  • Your chosen theme, wallpaper and text color, and wallpapers you make yourself
  • Which themes and wallpapers you have unlocked
  • Your Gold balance, what you have unlocked with it, and your remaining spins

We never see any of it. No server receives it — including your account, if you have one. It exists only in the app's storage on your phone. (The one exception is the short stats summary friends see, if you use Friends — see below.)

If you are signed in, the app also keeps a sign-in token from Unity in its private storage, so you stay signed in. It never stores your password on the device.

Saving wallpapers to your photos

In Themes → Background → Custom you can download a wallpaper you made. The app saves that one picture into your phone's photos — an album called "Magic 8-Ball" on iPhone, and the DCIM/Magic 8-Ball folder on Android.

  • On iPhone, the first time you do this your phone asks whether Magic 8-Ball may add photos. You can say no, or change your answer later in Settings.
  • On Android 9 and older, saving needs the storage permission, so the app declares it for those versions only. Android 10 and newer need no permission for this.

The app only adds the picture. It does not read or change your other photos, and nothing is uploaded — the wallpaper goes from the app's storage straight to your photos. Once saved, the picture belongs to your photos: it stays there if you reset or uninstall the app, until you delete it.

What the app sends, and to whom

The Movie Database (TMDB)

To show you films and TV shows, the app requests information from TMDB's public API. Those requests contain:

  • the text you type when you search — the Info page searches movies, TV shows and people at once
  • the identifier of a film, TV show, season or person whose page or streaming availability you are viewing
  • your chosen region, so that streaming availability is correct for your country

The app also downloads poster, backdrop, episode and profile images from TMDB's image service.

TMDB is an independent company and handles those requests under its own privacy policy, which we do not control: https://www.themoviedb.org/privacy-policy

This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB. Streaming availability data is provided by JustWatch.

Your library is not part of this. The app never sends your saved films or shows, your ratings, your reviews, your notes or the episodes you have watched to TMDB. Every request it makes to TMDB is a read — it asks for information and receives it.

Your account, if you create one (Unity Gaming Services)

Accounts are provided by Unity Gaming Services, run by Unity Technologies, which holds them on our behalf. What Unity receives depends on how you sign in:

  • Username and password: your username, and your password over an encrypted connection. Unity stores the password only in a form that cannot be read back.
  • Sign in with Apple (iPhone): an identifier Apple creates for you, and — only if you choose to share it in Apple's sign-in sheet — your email address or an Apple private relay address.
  • Google Play Games (Android): your Play Games player identifier.

Unity assigns your account a player ID and records when it was created. Its software also declares that it collects, for people who use an account:

  • an approximate location, which is not GPS — typically worked out from your internet connection
  • usage data about the sign-in service, used to run it and for Unity's analytics of that service
  • crash diagnostics, not linked to you

Unity handles all of this under its own privacy policy, which we do not control: https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy

If you sign in with Apple or Google, that company's own privacy policy also applies to the sign-in itself.

One account across our apps. The same account works in our other apps that use this sign-in: SYNDICATE, ARMORY LIBRARY and BLANK. Sign in to any of them with the same username, Apple or Google account and you are the same player in all of them.

What your account holds. Your Gold balance and what you have unlocked, so that a purchase made in one of those apps is available in the others you sign in to, with a record of which store purchases have already been credited, so none is credited twice. Gold and unlocks you had on this phone before you signed in are moved onto the account when you sign in. If you use Friends, it also holds the summary of your stats and the profile photo your friends see (see "Friends and chat" below). Signing in does not upload your library, your ratings, your reviews or your notes. We do not use your account for marketing.

Each of our other apps keeps its own data under its own privacy policy. If you use another of them, data that app keeps on your account is described there, not here.

Friends and chat, if you use them (Unity Gaming Services)

Friends and the chat are run by Unity: its Friends service for your friend name and friends list, its Vivox service for messages, and its Moderation service for reports. They work only with an account, and only between people who are friends.

What Unity receives and keeps:

  • your friend name — the name friends add you by
  • your friends list, friend requests, and the people you have blocked
  • your messages — what you type, and any movies or TV shows you share — and the messages your friends send you. Messages are kept by Unity's chat service so a chat can show earlier messages, including on another phone
  • when you report someone: who you reported and why
  • a summary of your stats, for your friends to compare with theirs (below)
  • your profile photo, if you choose one (below)

Your stats, for your friends. When you open Friends, the app saves a summary of your library to your account so your friends can compare it with theirs: how many films and TV shows are in your library, watched, favorites, watchlist and collection; how many reviews you have written; your average star rating; your most-watched title and how many times you have watched it; and the titles and years of the last five films or shows you marked watched. Only your friends can see it — Unity's servers check that you are friends before showing it to anyone. It never includes your notes, the text of your reviews, your collection details, or which episodes you watched and how you rated them.

Your profile photo, if you choose one. On the Account page you can pick a photo from your phone. The app crops it to a small square and keeps it on your account, and only your friends can see it — beside your name in their friends list, on your stats page and in your chats; Unity's servers check that you are friends before showing it. The app reads only the photo you pick, never the rest of your photos. You can remove it from the Account page at any time.

A shared movie or TV show carries public facts about it — title, year, poster, rating, director (or creator, for a show), genres and where it streams — and, if you have marked it watched, your star rating and review, which your friend sees with it. Never your private notes, your collection details, which of your lists it is on, or which episodes you watched.

So that shared movies and shows still look right when you reopen a chat, your phone also keeps a copy of each one shared in your chats — including the rating and review that came with it — in the app's own storage. It is deleted when you sign out.

You control your messages. You can edit or delete any message you sent; the change reaches your friend's copy too. "Clear chat" hides a conversation on your phone only — your friend keeps their copy of it.

Unity's chat software also declares that it collects an approximate location (typically from your internet connection), device and user identifiers, and diagnostic and performance data, to run the service. It is built for voice as well as text; this app never uses voice and never asks for your microphone.

Safety. You can block anyone, which ends the friendship and stops their messages and requests, and report anyone from the chat. Typed messages are filtered for offensive words. Reports are reviewed by us, and we may remove or ban accounts that break the rules.

Unity handles this data under its own privacy policy: https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy

Advertising (Unity LevelPlay)

Once you have used your free spins for the hour, the app can show you an optional ad in exchange for another spin. The ads are supplied by Unity through its LevelPlay service. The app does not contact the ad network before that point.

To show an ad and measure whether it was shown, the ad network receives:

  • your device's advertising identifier, where your phone provides one — a resettable ID for this purpose — and other device identifiers the network uses
  • your IP address, from which your approximate location (typically your city or region) can be derived
  • basic device information, such as model, operating system version and language
  • how you interact with an ad — whether it was shown, watched to the end, or tapped

The ads are not personalized. The app tells the ad network you have not consented to personalized advertising and that your information may not be sold, so the ads you see are chosen from the context rather than from a profile of you. On iPhone the app never asks for permission to track you, and does not track you.

Unity collects this information under its own privacy policy, which we do not control: https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy

Your movie library is never shared with advertisers. Your saved films and shows, ratings, reviews and notes are not sent to the ad network.

You can reset or delete your advertising identifier at any time in your device settings — on Android under Settings → Privacy → Ads, and on iPhone under Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Amazon links

A film's card, and its page in Info, have a Buy button with two choices: Buy disc and Rent or buy digital. Each opens a search on Amazon for the film's title — in Amazon's Movies & TV store, or its Prime Video store. Links to Prime Video for a film or show open Amazon too, and carry the same tag.

These are Amazon Associates links: they carry a tag that tells Amazon you came from this app, so we may earn a commission if you buy something. It does not change the price you pay. The app's Settings screen shows Amazon's disclosure: "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases."

The app itself sends nothing to Amazon. Tapping a button hands the link to your phone, which opens it in your browser (or in the Amazon app, if your phone sends Amazon links there). The link contains only what identifies the film or show (for Buy and Rent, its title), which store to look in, and our tag — never your library, your ratings, your account or anything else about you. Once you are on Amazon, Amazon's own privacy policy applies, which we do not control: https://www.amazon.com/privacy

You do not have to use these buttons; nothing else in the app depends on them.

Links out to streaming services and film sites

When you tap a streaming service, the app opens that service's app or website in the normal way, exactly as tapping a link would — or, when the app has no direct link for that title, a JustWatch page showing where it streams. The Info page also links to a title's pages on TMDB and IMDb. Once you are there, that company's own privacy policy applies. The app does not pass any information about you to them. (Prime Video links carry our Amazon Associates tag, as described above.)

What the app does not do

To be specific, because "we value your privacy" means nothing on its own. The app contains:

  • No ads you did not ask for — ads appear only when you choose to watch one for an extra spin, and they are not personalized
  • No analytics of our own and no crash or usage reporting to us. (Unity's sign-in service collects the data described above, and only for people who use an account.)
  • No tracking — nothing the app collects is used to follow you across other companies' apps or websites. The ad network receives your advertising identifier to show an ad (see "Advertising"), but not for personalized advertising
  • No required account — accounts are optional; they let you sign in, add friends and chat with them (see above)
  • No messages to strangers — the chat works only between friends, and you can block or report anyone
  • No payment details — buying Gold is handled entirely by Google Play or the App Store, and buying on Amazon is between you and Amazon. We never see your card or payment details; the app keeps a record of which Gold purchases it has already delivered, on your phone and, if you are signed in, on your account
  • No access to your location, camera, microphone, contacts or calendar. Your photos are touched only when you ask: the app reads the single photo you pick as a profile photo, and adds a wallpaper you choose to download. It never reads the rest of your photos

On Android, the app requests internet access and network state, to look up films and shows and to know when it is online; Google Play billing, which lets you buy Gold through Google Play; reading shared storage, which older versions of Android need to open the photo you pick as a profile photo (the app reads only that photo); writing shared storage, on Android 9 and older only, which those versions need to save a wallpaper you download into your photos (the app writes only that picture); and the advertising ID and Android ad services permissions, which the ad network uses as described above. The ad and billing libraries also declare permissions for scheduling their own background work (keeping the device awake briefly, running after a restart, a background service). Apart from the photo you pick and the wallpaper you save, none of these is used to reach your files, and none grants access to your contacts, location, camera or microphone. (The app also declares one permission scoped to itself, which Android requires of every modern app for its own internal messaging. It grants access to nothing on your device.)

On iPhone, if your phone asks you about photo access for Magic 8-Ball, it is only at the moment you pick a profile photo or save a wallpaper, and only for that.

Children

Magic 8-Ball is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. Because accounts can chat with friends, this matters: if you believe a child has created an account, contact us at the address below and we will delete it.

Your control over your data

Your movie and TV data stays on your device (apart from the stats summary your friends see, if you use Friends), so you are in complete control of it:

  • Reset the app from its Settings screen to clear your saved data.
  • Uninstall the app to remove all of it, permanently.

Wallpapers you have saved to your photos are not app data any more: they stay in your photos after a reset or uninstall, and you delete them there.

Deleting your account

  • In the app: open the menu → Account → Delete → Delete account. This permanently deletes your account from Unity Gaming Services straight away.
  • Without the app: email us at bryce.boynton@1stamendment.store with your username, or tell us you signed in with Apple or Google, and ask us to delete the account. We will delete it within 30 days and reply to confirm.

Deleting your account does not touch the movie and TV data on your phone. It removes your friend name, your friends list, the stats summary and profile photo your friends see, and the Gold and unlocks held on the account. Because the same account is used by our other apps listed above, deleting it ends it in all of them. Messages you already sent may remain in your friends' chat history, the way a sent text message stays on the other person's phone.

We do not sell personal information, and we tell the ad network it may not sell yours. We share account information, friends and messages only with Unity, which holds them on our behalf, and — if you choose to sign in with them — with Apple or Google as part of that sign-in. Ad data goes to Unity's ad service only when you watch an ad. Amazon receives only what your browser sends when you choose to open one of its links.

Security

Requests to TMDB, to Unity and to the ad network are made over encrypted HTTPS connections, and the Amazon links are HTTPS too. Your password is never stored on your device, and Unity stores it only in a form that cannot be read back. Your library is stored in the app's private storage, protected by your device's own operating system and its security features.

Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy — for example if analytics or library sync is added — this document will be updated and the date at the top changed before that version is released.
